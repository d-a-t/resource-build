## Budget staples for college students

- **Starches & Grains:** Jasmine rice, rolled oats, sweet potatoes, and russet potatoes are cheap ($0.50–$1.20/lb), filling, and safe.
- **Proteins:** Fresh skinless chicken breast, ground turkey, or lean beef ($3.50–$6.00/lb). Buy in family packs at grocery stores and freeze single portions immediately upon returning to your dorm or apartment.
- **Produce:** Carrots, zucchini, broccoli, cucumbers, apples, and blueberries ($1.50–$2.50/lb). Plain unseasoned frozen vegetables are great emergency dorm backups.
- **Eggs:** Fresh eggs (if tolerated) are an affordable, quick-cooking dorm protein ($0.25–$0.35 each).

## Atlanta grocery transit for Georgia Tech students

- **Publix at Atlantic Station (1380 Atlantic Dr NW):**
  - **Transit:** Board the **Georgia Tech Stinger Gold Route** (free for students with BuzzCard) from the Transit Hub or Ferst Dr; get off right at Atlantic Station.
  - **Best For:** Consistent fresh chicken breasts, whole produce, and standard pantry staples.
- **Trader Joe's Midtown (931 Monroe Dr NE):**
  - **Transit:** Accessible via MARTA Bus 36 or a quick 10-minute bike ride via the Eastside BeltLine / 10th St bike lane.
  - **Best For:** Affordable oats, frozen blueberries, olive oil, and inexpensive root vegetables.
- **Kroger (1715 Howell Mill Rd NW):**
  - **Transit:** Short rideshare or GT Stinger Red Route transfer.
  - **Best For:** Bulk family packs of chicken and ground turkey at lowest per-pound prices.
- **Whole Foods Market Midtown (22 14th St NW):**
  - **Transit:** Walkable from East Campus / Tech Square (approx. 10–12 minute walk north on Spring St).
  - **Best For:** High-grade specialty oils, unseasoned fresh meats, and certified organic produce.

## Dorm cooking & mini-fridge freezer management

Cooking in Georgia Tech residence halls (e.g. Glenn, Towers, Smith, Folk, Armstrong, Center Street, or North Ave) requires working around space and appliance limits:

1. **Permitted Appliances:**
   - GT Housing permits microwaves (up to 1,000 watts), electric kettles with auto shutoff, and rice cookers in dorm rooms.
   - Traditional halls have communal kitchens on select floors; North Ave Apartments and ULC have in-unit full kitchens.

2. **The "Cook & Freeze" Protocol:**
   - Histamine accumulates rapidly in cooked meats and leftover dishes held at refrigerator temperatures ($40^\circ\text{F}$).
   - **Do not meal-prep for the whole week in the fridge.** Instead, divide cooked meals into single-serving freezer-safe silicone or ziplock bags.
   - Flatten bags so they freeze quickly and stack neatly inside standard dorm mini-fridge freezer compartments.
   - Defrost or reheat directly from frozen in the microwave. Never let cooked food sit at room temperature.

3. **Dorm-Friendly Equipment Hacks:**
   - **Rice Cooker:** Cook rice on the bottom while steaming chicken strips, carrots, or zucchini in the top basket simultaneously.
   - **Microwave:** Poke holes in potatoes or sweet potatoes and microwave for 5–7 minutes; prepare quick oatmeal with fresh apple slices and cinnamon in 2 minutes.
   - **Store-Bought Broth Warning:** Commercial shelf-stable chicken broths and bouillon cubes often contain yeast extract, autolyzed proteins, and MSG (histamine liberators). Use plain filtered water, olive oil, fresh garlic, and salt for soup bases.
