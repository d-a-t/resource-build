## Low-cost staples

- Rice, oats, and potatoes (including sweet potatoes) are cheap, filling, and usually well tolerated.
- Fresh or frozen chicken, turkey, or beef. Buy in bulk and portion it before freezing.
- Eggs, if you tolerate them.
- Seasonal vegetables such as carrots, zucchini, and broccoli. Plain frozen vegetables are a good backup.

## Batch cooking and freezing

Histamine builds up as cooked food sits. Cook a batch, divide it into single portions, and freeze within about an hour of cooking *[VERIFY timing against a source]*. Reheat a portion straight from the freezer. Avoid repeated cooling and reheating.

## Methods that work in a dorm or small apartment

- **Rice cooker:** cooks rice, steams vegetables on top, and can cook rice with small chicken pieces.
- **Microwave:** bakes potatoes and sweet potatoes, cooks oatmeal, and steams vegetables.
- **One pot or one pan:** soups, skillets, and hashes need only a pot or pan and a hot plate or stove.
- **Broth note:** store-bought broth and bouillon are often high in histamine. Use water, salt, and fresh herbs instead.
